01 — THE ORIGINS

Painted in caves.
Carried through time.

Hidden within the basalt cliffs of Maharashtra, the Ajanta Caves preserve a remarkable world of Buddhist art—where gestures, expressions, and colour carry stories beyond words.

Among the traditions represented in Buddhist storytelling are the Jātaka tales: accounts of the Buddha’s previous births, each offering a reflection on how to live with wisdom and compassion.

c. 2nd century BCE – 5th/6th century CEAjanta, Maharashtra, India
“

Greatness is not measured by the power one holds,
but by the care one gives.

SCENE 01THE FOREST SANCTUARY
THE PROMISE · HIMALAYAN FOREST

A quiet promise

In a peaceful forest lived a noble white elephant, the Bodhisattva. His aged parents were blind, so each day he searched for tender leaves, fruits, and lotus stalks to nourish them.

“As long as I have strength, they will never be left uncared for.”
THE LESSONLove is a daily act of care.
SCENE 02A STRANGER IN NEED
THE ENCOUNTER · A MOUNTAIN PATH

A stranger on the path

One day, the elephant found a weary traveller lost among the forest ridges. Instead of turning away, he carried the stranger safely through the wilderness and guided him towards the road.

“Climb on. I will help you find your way.”
THE LESSONKindness asks for no reward.
SCENE 03THE KING'S FORESTERS
THE BETRAYAL · THE ROYAL FOREST

Trust betrayed

In Varanasi, the traveller learned that the king needed a new state elephant. Tempted by reward, he revealed the elephant’s refuge. The elephant was captured, but his thoughts remained with his helpless parents.

“I offered help with an open heart. Why has it brought sorrow?”
THE LESSONBetrayal can wound the innocent.

◉ ◉ ◉
SCENE 04THE ROYAL COURTYARD
THE REALISATION · VARANASI

A silent answer

At the palace, rich food was placed before the elephant, but he refused to eat. His sorrow spoke louder than words. When the king listened and learned about the blind parents left behind, his heart was moved.

“What feast can comfort a heart that fears for its family?”
THE LESSONTrue leadership begins with listening.
✿　✿　✿
SCENE 05HOME, AT LAST
THE RETURN · FIRST LIGHT

The way home

The king recognised that compassion was worth more than royal possession. He set the elephant free. At dawn, the elephant returned to the old Sal tree and embraced his parents with his trunk.

“I am home. You are safe. That is all I wished for.”
THE LESSONCompassion is the truest form of nobility.

03 — THE MEANING

A tale with
many lives.

The Matri-Poshaka Jātaka is more than an animal story. It asks what we owe to those who depend on us—and what power should look like when guided by empathy.

❋
01 / CARE

Filial devotion

Honouring and caring for parents and elders, especially when they are vulnerable.

♡
02 / KINDNESS

Help without reward

Offering support because another being needs it—not because we expect something back.

⌁
03 / INTEGRITY

The cost of betrayal

Remembering that greed can break trust and place innocent lives at risk.

☼
04 / WISDOM

Empathetic leadership

Listening before judging, and choosing justice over possession or pride.

04 — LIVING HERITAGE

Walls that
still speak.

Ajanta’s paintings are precious records of artistic skill, spiritual ideas, clothing, architecture, and everyday life in ancient India. Their storytelling tradition turns a wall into a world—one that can still teach us today.

Digital storytelling offers a new way to encounter this heritage, while careful preservation protects the fragile originals for generations to come.
